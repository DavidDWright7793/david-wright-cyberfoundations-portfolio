## Recommended controls
### SC-03
- **Control:** One control that the clinic could implement with the staff laptops is to make sure that they are never left unattended \(as in there is at least one person in the room with the laptops at all times\), and if someone steps away from their workstation, make sure they lock their screen.
- **How it helps:** If the workspace with the staff laptops is never left completely unattended and staff always lock the screens of their laptops before stepping away from them, unauthorized individuals are less likely to access the laptops in order to get information from them or steal them.
- **Risk remaining afterwards:** The laptops are still relatively vulnerable to being stolen or at least illicitly accessed, as they are in a workspace that is right next to a hallway that is regularly traversed by patients and other non-employees. Having at least one employee in the workspace at all times and having all employees lock their screens when they step away from their laptops makes it less likely a threat actor will access the company laptops, but the fact the workspace is so accessible to the general public leaves some risk.

### SC-05
- **Control:** One control that the clinic could implement with the backup drive is to back up the information on it on more regular and frequent intervals, regularly perform restoration tests on the data, and make copies of the drive and store them in other areas \(such as offsite or in the cloud\).
- **How it helps:** If the backup drive has its data backed up more regularly and frequently, has restoration tests performed regularly on its data, and has copies stored in other locations \(offsite, in the cloud\), the data is less likely to be deleted/lost and then unable to be recovered.
- **Risk remaining afterwards:** Properly managing the data on the backup drive makes it less likely that the data could be lost and never recovered, but the fact that the backup drive is permanently plugged into the reception workstation \(one of the most accessible areas for non-staff\) still makes it relatively easy for unauthorized individuals to steal it and/or gain sensitive/confidential data from it.

One control I recommend that your clinic implement is that at least one employee should be required to be the workspace that contains the clinic-owned laptops at all times, and all employees should be required to lock their screens when they step away from their laptops. This makes the laptops less likely to be accessed or stolen by unauthorized individuals, though there is still the possibility of that happening due to the laptops being in a workspace that is easily accessible to non-staff. Another control I recommend is more responsible management of data on the backup drive-data on it should be backed up more regularly and frequently, restoration tests should be performed \(and documented\) at regular intervals, and there should be copies of the backup drive stored in other locations \(such as offsite or in the cloud\). These controls would make it less likely that the data on the backup drive could be lost and not recovered, but the fact the backup drive is permanently plugged into the reception workstation \(a very accessible area for non-staff\) still makes it \(and the data it contains\) susceptible to being accessed or stolen by unauthorized individuals.
